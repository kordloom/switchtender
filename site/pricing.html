Questions

Which tier do I need?

Community, until one policy stops being enough. When production needs a rule of its own, or people should sign in from the directory you already run, that is Pro. The week you need deny rules or agent-scoped policy, PostgreSQL behind more than one controller, workers reaching fleets a single machine cannot, or a change register an auditor will actually read, that is Team. Enterprise is for when the proof has to come from outside your own install: a KordLoom-operated witness, custody of your evidence, and evidence work on your audit calendar. Most organizations should start on Community and let the gate tell them when they have outgrown it.

Can I move from AWX?

Yes, on Community, in one command, and the same goes for Semaphore, Rundeck, Jenkins freestyle jobs, and plain crontabs. AAP, Tower, and Ascender share the AWX export format. For a large or regulated move, the Migration Program does it for you. Start with the switching guide.

I started on SQLite. How do I get to Postgres and HA?

Team includes Postgres. Today the supported move is switchtender backup and restore, which carries your configuration: projects, templates, inventories, credentials with their sealed secrets, schedules, and tokens. Run history and the audit chain stay with the SQLite file, and that is not a loss: the old database remains readable and its evidence verifiable forever, because receipts never depend on where the data lives now. If you want the history physically merged, tell us and we will do the move with you.

How do I buy or trial today?

Email licensing@switchtender.com with your org name and band. A signed 30-day trial license usually lands within hours, no card. Buying works the same way with an invoice until card checkout ships. Enterprise starts with a conversation.

Do you count my hosts?

No. A host is an inventory entry that received a run in the prior twelve months, counted once however many inventories name it: dead inventory costs nothing. Bands are self-reported at purchase, the binary counts nothing and reports nothing, and we will never ask to audit. Growth mid-term costs nothing: if your fleet outgrows its band, you move up at your next renewal, and that trust is priced in.

What happens when a license lapses?

Paid features stop at their next license check, with no restart, and nothing is deleted, locked, or held. Your data, your evidence, your receipts, and every Community feature keep working, and a database you initialized on PostgreSQL stays fully usable forever. A few things deliberately keep going. Approval policies keep enforcing, advanced rules included, because a gate that opened on the day a term lapsed would release the runs it was written to hold. Directory sign-in stays on until the server next starts, then falls back to local accounts. The policy file still reloads, so rules the API would refuse on a lapsed license can still be added there, and servers already sharing a PostgreSQL schema keep sharing it. We built the gate so that it cannot hold your data hostage even if we wanted it to.

What support comes with each tier?

Community uses the public issue tracker. Pro adds email support, with an engineer's first response within two business days. On Team, Severity 1 and Severity 2 reports, production runs blocked or a paid feature materially degraded with no workaround, get a first response within one business day, and Severity 1 is worked first. The rest is reasonable efforts. Enterprise puts an SLA on the order form, with its coverage and clocks stated there. Severities, business days, and what a first response means are defined in the terms, so the commitment is checkable rather than a feeling.

Why is the change register paid when receipts are free?

Because proofs and packaging are different things. Anyone must be able to verify what your install did, on any tier, forever, or the evidence story is worthless, so the chain, anchors, receipts, dossiers, and verifier are all Community. The period change register is the assembled compliance deliverable an auditor consumes, and assembling deliverables is a thing organizations pay for.

What does the license allow?

SwitchTender is source-available under BSL 1.1. You can read the source, modify it, self-host it, and run it in production for your own organization. The restriction is offering it as a competing hosted service. Each release converts to Apache 2.0 two years after it ships. See the LICENSE for the exact terms.

What exactly is the license file?

A small signed JSON document. The binary carries our public key and verifies the signature offline. Nothing is fetched, ever. Install it with switchtender license install, see where you stand with switchtender license status. No license server, no activation, no phone-home, and losing internet changes nothing.

What happens to paid features if you disappear?

Your license keeps verifying, because verification is offline, so everything works through the end of your term, and nothing you produced is ever lost: Community, your data, and your receipts work forever, and the open verifier does not depend on us. Each release also converts to Apache 2.0 two years after it ships, and the terms accelerate that: if KordLoom ceases business, every published release converts immediately. The code itself outlives the company, on a clock you can enforce.
